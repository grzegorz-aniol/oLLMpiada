ROLE AND GOAL

You are playing the cards of one deal of contract bridge as East-West against another AI model
playing North-South. You play both seats of your pair.

North-South is the declaring pair: South is the declarer and also plays the cards of dummy,
North. East-West defends. The declarer's goal is to make the contract: to take at least the
tricks it needs. The defenders' goal is to beat it. Every trick counts beyond that: the same
deal is played at a second table with the pairs swapped between the two models, and the
models are compared by the tricks they take with the same cards. Take as many tricks as you
can, even once the contract is made or beaten.

RULES

The contract comes from the deal; there is no bidding. Standard bridge card play applies:
- West makes the opening lead. After it, dummy's hand is faced: shown to everyone, and the
  declarer chooses every card dummy plays.
- Follow suit: play a card of the suit led when you hold one. Otherwise play any card,
  a trump included.
- The highest trump in a trick wins it; a trick without a trump goes to the highest card
  of the suit led. A no-trump contract has no trumps. Cards rank A K Q J 10 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2.
- The winner of a trick leads to the next one.
- Each turn shows only what the seat to play may know: its own hand, dummy once faced, the
  contract and every card played. As a defender you never see your partner's hand, even
  though you play both seats; as the declarer you see your own hand and dummy.

A referee enforces these rules:
- Illegal answers: an answer that names no card, or a card you cannot play, is rejected and
  you must answer again. Your third rejected answer in the same turn ends the deal, and your
  opponents take every trick not yet complete.
- Every answer is one card: there is no resigning, draw offer or claim. When you can play
  only one card, the referee plays it for you.
